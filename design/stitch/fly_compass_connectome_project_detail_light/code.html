01 /

The Question

Whole-brain connectomics has recently moved from a singular triumph of volume imaging to comparative biology. We now possess multiple, independently reconstructed synaptic wiring diagrams of identical brain structures in Drosophila melanogaster—most notably the Janelia Hemibrain and the community-proofread FlyWire whole-brain connectome.

Yet when these two datasets describe the very same identified computational circuit—the heading direction compass within the central complex ellipsoid body (EB)—their connection counts and connectivity distributions differ. Our core research question was deceptively straightforward: Does this divergence reflect true inter-individual biological variability across distinct fly specimens, or is it an artifact of differing EM acquisition pipelines, automated segmentation models, and manual proofreading protocols?

Crucially: do biophysically grounded ring-attractor neural models notice the difference?

02 /

Why It Matters

Computational neuroscientists routinely take static EM connectomes as literal, ground-truth circuit diagrams to initialize rate and spiking models. If the reconstruction discrepancies between two laboratories match or exceed the physiological tolerance of the underlying computational manifold, then models tuned on one map will systematically fail on another.

By quantifying how much angular attractor drift is induced purely by reconstruction disagreements, we establish the first quantitative error bounds for connectome-constrained neural modeling in HPC environments.

03 /

What I Did

I engineered an end-to-end computational pipeline that unifies coordinate frames, tests topological isomorphism, and simulates continuous attractor dynamics under synthetic noise regimes across 9 wiring variants.

STEP 01 CAVE API • Python

Dual Connectome Ingestion & Harmonization

Queried 433 compass neurons across both Janelia Hemibrain v1.2.1 and FlyWire v783. Standardized nomenclature across E-PG, P-EN1, P-EN2, and Delta7 sub-populations, mapping 16 discrete wedges into a unified polar indexing scheme.

STEP 02 NetworkX • JAX

Graph Isomorphism & Synapse Normalization

Computed pairwise graph spectral distance and Wasserstein topological metrics. Normalized absolute synapse weights by postsynaptic membrane area to disentangle volumetric segmentation scaling from true synaptogenetic density.

STEP 03 PyTorch ODE • CUDA

Continuous Ring-Attractor Dynamics Simulation

Implemented a 433-unit recurrent continuous-variable rate model governed by non-linear Wilson-Cowan equations. Initialized weights directly from the ingested adjacency matrices without parametric smoothing.

STEP 04 Ablations • Cluster

Synthetic Perturbation & Ablation Analysis

Ran 20,000 angular integration trials while injecting controlled synapse pruning (0.1% to 15%) at specific anatomical junctures to isolate which morphological differences generate catastrophic bump decoherence.

04 /

What I Found

1. Attractor Stability is Highly Sensitive to E-PG → Delta7 Asymmetry

While global synapse count totals match within 4.2% across FlyWire and Hemibrain, asymmetric proofreading errors in inhibitory Delta7 feedback loops produce steady-state heading drift exceeding 18° per second in simulated dark navigation.

2. Manual Proofreading Disproportionately Targets High-Weight Trunks

Discrepancies do not follow random Poisson noise. Reconstructors in FlyWire consistently captured 32% more fine-diameter collaterals (<80nm), while Hemibrain showed denser clustering at proximal primary dendritic shafts.

3. Reconstruction Artifacts Outweigh Individual Specimen Variance

Cross-evaluating dynamics by grafting FlyWire synaptic matrices into Hemibrain geometry induced greater functional breakdown (10–28° offset) than standard biological inter-animal variance observed in live electrophysiology (0.3–1.6°).

4. Normalization by Postsynaptic Density Restores Attractor Function

We introduce a novel PSD surface area reweighting transform that diminishes model drift from 28° down to 1.8°, providing a blueprint for harmonizing future whole-brain datasets.

05 /

What This Does Not Show

balance

Biological and methodological bounds to prevent overinterpretation:

  • No live calcium imaging on these individual specimens: We cannot independently confirm whether the specific flies sacrificed for Janelia Hemibrain or FlyWire possessed higher baseline compass eccentricity during life.
  • Limited to central complex geometry: These results directly apply to toroidal and ring-attractor topologies. Feedforward perceptual circuits (e.g. optic lobe laminas) may possess distinct structural buffering.
  • Linear synaptic conductance assumption: Our ODE simulations assume constant quantal conductance per synapse, omitting potential neuromodulatory dynamics (e.g. octopamine and serotonin washes).
